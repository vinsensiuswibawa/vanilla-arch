## Studio and performance decisions (2026-10-06)

- Lighting: Poly Haven `studio_small_09` (CC0) at 1K (1.6 MB .hdr), intensity 1.5, rotated 60°; no live
  ambient occlusion (it cost 20–30 fps for a subtle gain). Tested in `/lab` (dev only).
- Materials: keep each model's authored materials and textures. Paint is replaced by the listing's colour as
  clear-coat paint; near-opaque exported glass is made see-through; transmission is replaced by clear glossy
  glass (it renders the scene a second time per frame).
- Shadow: baked once per car from a top-down silhouette (two blur radii), on a quad that turns with the car.
- Geometry: 110k triangles per car with protected shares (paint 38%, wheels and tyres 22%); smooth normals are
  recomputed on paint only.
- Measured on an Intel Arc 130T laptop at 1440×900 (production-equivalent lab): 60 fps for both test cars at
  1× and 2× pixel density, still 60 with each frame rendered 3–4× (GPU headroom), and 60 with the CPU throttled
  4×. At 6× CPU throttling the Porsche drops to 43 fps (75 separate meshes): join meshes by material in the
  asset pass.

## Shipping pipeline (2026-10-07)

`npm run models` (`scripts/models/optimize.mjs`) builds all 13 from `content/lineup.json` and `raw/<slug>.glb`
(`npm run models:fetch` downloads them). It writes the hero file as `public/models/<slug>.glb` (400k triangles,
2K textures; the user accepted hero quality for the desktop showcase) and `<slug>.lod1.glb`, and bakes the lab's
material repairs into both: transmission replaced by clear or tinted glossy lenses, the Gallardo's opaque
`glass_trans` made see-through, matte tyres, no clear coat on black trim and interiors, near-opaque glass made
see-through. Only the paint is applied at runtime (`lib/materials.ts`), from the listing. `--lab` adds the 110k
budget file to `public/lab` for comparisons. The showroom, studio and lab share one studio look
(`components/three/StudioLook.tsx`: `studio_small_09` at 1K from `public/hdri`, dark floor, overhead softbox, two
side strips, rendered once per renderer) and the baked shadow (`components/three/BakedShadow.tsx`), under ACES.
Total for the 13 hero files: see `docs/handoff.md`.

## Asset pass (2026-10-06, superseded above)

All 15 models were processed by `scripts/models/lab.mjs` (now `optimize.mjs`) from the settings in `content/lineup.json` (props,
orientation, real length, paint material, protected detail) into three files each, reviewed in `/lab`:

| File | Use | Cap | 15 cars total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `<slug>.budget.glb` | Showroom row, up to four cars live | 110k triangles, 1K textures (Montreal 220k) | 21.4 MB |
| `<slug>.hero.glb` | The one car on an open listing | 400k triangles, 2K textures | 36.3 MB |
| `<slug>.lod1.glb` | Phones, one live car | 40k triangles, 512 px textures (Montreal 70k) | 10.6 MB |

- Meshes are joined by material: 12–50 draw calls per car (the MX-5 has 13).
- Every reduced car was compared raw against budget at the reference angle. The Montreal (wire grille, spoked
  wheels) and the Datsun (chrome lettering) needed protection; the Montreal's grille keeps full detail in its
  hero file.
- Measured at 60 fps on the Intel Arc laptop for the heaviest cases: Montreal hero (419k) at 1× and 2× pixel
  density and with every frame drawn twice; Datsun hero at 2×; Montreal budget, MX-5 and TR6 with frames drawn
  3–4×.
- Not yet done: moving the files into `public/models`, new listings in `lib/cars.ts`, photo sets, and emblems for
  the new makes (Porsche, Triumph, Fiat, Peugeot, Ferrari, Subaru, Alfa Romeo, Lotus, Datsun).
